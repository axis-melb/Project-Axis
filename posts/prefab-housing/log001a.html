The Question Most People Never Ask

Here's a question worth asking: does everyone know you need a building permit to add a deck to your backyard? Or a pod? Most people don't — until they're selling the property and it becomes someone else's problem.

This isn't a gap in legislation. The NCC exists. Building regulations exist. Planning schemes exist. The framework is there, and it's detailed.

The real issue is two things: enforcement and public literacy.

Everyone knows speeding gets you a fine. That awareness didn't happen by accident — it's decades of consistent enforcement, signage, cameras, and consequences. Building compliance doesn't have that same cultural weight. An owner can place a non-compliant structure in their backyard and nothing happens — until it does.

Prefab sits in the middle of this.

The Code Is Method-Neutral

One of the most commonly misunderstood points in the Australian prefab industry is this: NCC 2022 does not treat prefabricated construction as a separate category requiring special dispensation.

A volumetric module — a three-dimensional structural unit manufactured off-site and assembled on-site — is assessed against the same performance requirements as any other building element. The code doesn't care where the wall was built. It cares whether the wall performs.

This means a prefabricated module must satisfy the same compliance pathways as a conventionally constructed equivalent:

DTS
Deemed-to-Satisfy — the module meets the NCC's specific technical provisions directly. The most straightforward path for standardised volumetric products.
PERFORMANCE
Performance Solution — compliance is demonstrated through evidence: expert judgment, comparative assessment, or verification methods. More appropriate for innovative or non-standard systems.
COMBO
Combination — elements of both, applied to different parts of the same building. Common where prefab modules integrate with conventional structural systems.

Note: NCC 2025 came into effect in May 2026. Based on current indications, changes to volumetric module provisions are not expected to be as significant as the 2022 revision. A follow-up note will be published at a later stage.

The Visibility Problem

The structures that get photographed and shared online are rarely the compliant ones. Social media is full of backyard container conversions and prefab installs with no permits or approvals, because those are the projects that attract attention. The compliant bathroom pods and properly approved modular builds almost never get posted — even though they make up the quieter, legitimate side of the industry.

That's the image prefab is fighting against — and it's not a regulation problem. It's a visibility problem.

Done properly, through the right channels, prefab components comply with the same NCC requirements as any other build. The engineering is assessed. The permits are pulled. It's construction, not a loophole.

The off-site production environment actually creates an opportunity for better compliance documentation — factory QA systems, material traceability, dimensional consistency — that on-site construction rarely achieves consistently.
